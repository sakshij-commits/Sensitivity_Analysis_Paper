Final Research Title:
Sensitivity Analysis of Carbon-Aware Scheduling in Cloud Computing

Technology Stack:

Existing Research Work:
Existing research has studied carbon-aware cloud scheduling through temporal and geographical workload shifting, carbon-intensity-based scheduling, workload flexibility, carbon-intensity forecasting, and optimization of carbon emissions along with cost and performance. Studies have demonstrated that carbon-aware scheduling can reduce emissions, but the reported benefits vary across workloads, regions, carbon-intensity profiles and scheduling constraints.

Identified Research Gap:
Existing studies evaluate carbon-aware scheduling under different experimental conditions and methodologies. As a result, reported carbon-saving percentages are not always directly comparable. There is a need for a systematic sensitivity analysis using a common evaluation framework to determine how changes in workload characteristics, carbon-intensity conditions and scheduling flexibility affect the estimated carbon-saving benefits.

Research Objectives:

To review existing carbon-aware scheduling approaches and their evaluation methodologies.
To identify key experimental factors that influence reported carbon-saving results.
To develop a common framework for evaluating carbon-aware scheduling.
To analyze the effect of workload characteristics, carbon-intensity profiles and scheduling flexibility on estimated carbon savings.
To quantify and compare the sensitivity of carbon-saving results to these factors and identify the factors with the greatest influence.
